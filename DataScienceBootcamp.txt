1. How do you assess the statistical significance of an insight?
You determine whether or not the outcome is probably the result of chance. 
Typically, a p-value is computed to accomplish this. A p-value of less than
0.05 indicates that the outcome is statistically significant and unlikely 
to have occurred by accident.

2. What is the Central Limit Theorem? Explain it. Why is it important?
According to the Central Limit Theorem, even if the initial data is not normal,
the average of a large enough sample will have a normal (bell-shaped) curve. 
It's crucial since it enables us to apply standard techniques for a variety of data kinds.

3. What is the statistical power?
The likelihood that a test will accurately identify a true effect is known as statistical 
power. A study is good at identifying differences when they actually exist if it has high power. 
It relies on factors like the size of the effect and the sample size.

4. How do you control for biases?
One way to mitigate bias is to randomly assign participants to a research.
Employing control groups.
Blinding, or concealing information from participants

Thoroughly cleaning your data.
Making adjustments for additional analytic variables.

5. What are confounding variables?
Something that influences both the object of the test and the result is known as a confusing variable. 
It may lead you to believe that there is a relationship when in fact there isn't. For instance, 
swimming and ice cream sales can both rise in hot weather, giving the impression that consuming 
ice cream induces swimming.

6. What is A/B testing?
A/B testing is the process of comparing two iterations of a product 
to determine which performs better. One group is shown one version at random, 
and another group is shown the other version. The outcomes are then compared.

7. What are confidence intervals?
A confidence interval gives a range where you expect the true answer to fall. 
Like a 95% confidence interval means you’re 95% sure the true value is in that range. 
It helps you understand how exact your estimate is.
